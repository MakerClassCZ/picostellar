# How picostellar works

## The three techniques

- **picogame** draws the frame: a `Scene` with one `StripDraw` layer, so the
  sky is rendered strip by strip into the engine's own buffers and no
  screen-sized buffer exists; labels and bodies use `Canvas` primitives and
  `Canvas.text` on the strip view. The same code runs on the Fruit Jam's DVI
  framebuffer and on SPI panels (PicoPad with an RP2040 or an RP2350).
- **turbo** (`lib/skyviper.<arch>.mpy`): the star projection, the strip
  bucketing, the dot plotting and the line drawing are `@micropython.viper`
  kernels compiled to machine code. The same loops in plain Python
  (`skykernel.py`) are the fallback and the reference: both produce identical
  pixels (`tools/kernel_check.py` proves it on the board). A full redraw of
  the whole-sky view (810 stars, 743 lines, labels) takes about 60 ms on an
  RP2350 including the SPI transfer; the Python twin needs seconds.
- **xip_map** (`storage.map_file`): the 90 KB catalogue is read straight from
  flash. The firmware maps the file and hands the kernels memoryviews into
  it, so no RAM ever holds the star data.

## Animation level

Zooms glide and the sky follows a held D-pad. Frames drawn while the sky moves
are lighter than the full chart (no names, and below "full" only the bright
stars, to magnitude 4), and the full chart follows when it stops.

| level | moving frames | zoom glide |
|---|---|---|
| off | none: every step is a full chart | jump |
| light | bright stars and horizon, lines not smoothed | 4 frames |
| smooth | bright stars, constellation lines, horizon | 8 frames |
| full | every star, constellation lines, horizon | 8 frames |

The level is picked from how long the first full chart takes.

| board | full chart | moving: light | smooth | full | auto picks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PicoPad, RP2040 | 99-112 ms | 42-57 ms | 58-88 ms | 95-103 ms | light |
| PicoPad 2, RP2350 | 63-66 ms | 46-47 ms | 50-53 ms | 61-66 ms | smooth |
| Fruit Jam, RP2350 | 50-55 ms | 17-26 ms | 29-45 ms | 49-53 ms | smooth |

On the PicoPad 2 most of a frame is the SPI transfer to the display, about
40 ms.

## Drawing

Lines are anti-aliased (Xiaolin Wu): each step covers the two pixels across
the line, weighted by how close the line passes, so the chart has no
staircases, even blown up to a full-screen video. Stars are round dots with a
bright core and a soft rim, drawn after the lines; a dot only brightens what
is under it, so it never cuts a line. Smoothing costs 1-9 ms a frame on an
RP2350; light moving frames skip it, it starts off on boards without the
native kernels, and "Smooth lines" in the menu switches it.

Every line segment is filed under the render strips it crosses
(`bucket_lines`), so each strip draws its lines and then its stars in one
kernel call (`draw_strip`); stars below the horizon are dropped in the
projection. The horizon and the ecliptic are fixed circles: a time step only
turns their two axes into the catalogue frame and `ring` places the 36 points,
so stepping the clock costs about 13 ms on an RP2350, most of it the Sun, Moon
and planets. Names work the same way: `visible` picks the projected names and
constellations that land on screen, so Python touches only the labels it
draws.

The cursor is a picogame Sprite over the StripDraw sky, so moving it repaints
only the rectangles it left and entered: a jump costs about 30 ms on an
RP2040 against about 140 ms for a full repaint. The card and the menu repaint
their own rectangles through `StripDraw.invalidate`, and the nearest-object
searches run in the viper kernels (`pick_dir`, `pick_near`).

## How a frame is drawn

`redraw()` runs once per change: the kernels project the catalogue into a
display list of (x, y, size, colour) records (at most 2,500, 20 KB), bucket it
by render strip, project the line vertices and compute each segment's row
range. `scene.refresh()` then calls the `StripDraw` callback for every strip:
it clears a strip-sized scratch buffer, draws the segments whose row range
touches the strip, plots the strip's star buckets on top, blits the scratch
into the strip and draws the labels and bodies whose rows it holds with Canvas
calls. RAM: scratch 5 KB (8 rows) or 10 KB (16 rows on a framebuffer display
such as the Jam's), two display lists 24 KB, line buckets 5 KB, projected
vertices and bounds 6 KB; the catalogue itself stays in flash.

On a Fruit Jam the heap runs on from SRAM into PSRAM, and the strip scratch is
cleared, drawn and copied in every strip. In PSRAM that made a full chart take
99 ms instead of 57 ms, so `skybuffer` allocates the scratch, the sorted
display list, the line buckets and the projected vertices before anything
else.

## Astronomy

Yale Bright Star Catalogue 5, constellation lines, Latin names and ranks from
d3-celestial, star names from the IAU Working Group on Star Names, and each
star's constellation from the IAU boundaries (computed on the host with
pyephem); `tools/sky_pack.py` packs them into `sky.bin` (90 KB).

Positions are good to a few arc minutes; time is kept as whole days plus a
fraction because a Julian day in a 32-bit float would be rounded to six hours.
The catalogue is J2000 and the Sun, Moon and planets are of date: the view
folds the precession between the two (IAU 1976) into the rotation matrix the
kernels already apply, so the stars move to the date at no per-star cost
(0.32 deg in 2026).

## Building the drive image

`tools/build_circuitpy.py` regenerates `CIRCUITPY/` from the sources in the
repository root: it compiles the app and the six modules with `mpy-cross -O2`,
copies the catalogue, the icon and the native kernels, and takes the three
picogame helpers from a picogame-libs checkout (`--picogame-libs DIR`). The
app and the modules ship compiled because compiling on the board costs time
and heap: on an RP2040 the app takes 1.6 s from source against 0.5 s from
`.mpy` and leaves 4 KB less heap. An edited `code.py` copied over the stub,
or an edited module copied
into `lib/` in place of its `.mpy`, runs without `mpy-cross` at that cost.
`skyviper.py` must stay off the board: it would shadow the native `.mpy`, and
the board cannot compile viper code.

Without native `.mpy` support the board still loads the cross-compiled
bytecode; only the `skyviper` kernels need it, and `skykernel.py` steps in.
Without `storage.map_file` the catalogue is read into RAM.

## Tools

| tool | what it does |
|---|---|
| `tools/build_circuitpy.py` | regenerates `CIRCUITPY/` |
| `tools/sky_pack.py` | builds `sky.bin` from `data/` (needs `ephem`) |
